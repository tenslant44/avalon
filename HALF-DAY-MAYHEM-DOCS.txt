HALF-DAY MAYHEM
PROJECT DOCUMENTATION
Updated: 2026-10-25

1. RUNNING THE PROJECT
----------------------
Open index.html in Websim's preview. There is no build step or package install.
The game uses Three.js 0.170.0 and cannon-es 0.20.0 through CDN imports.
qa.html?qa=1 runs the acceptance suite; qa.html?visual=1 runs the visual fixture.
Tests are defined in game.js and report individual results plus a final count.

2. CONTROLS
-----------
WASD / arrows: camera-relative movement.
Mouse / right-drag: look and aim. Board and side views use the screen pointer.
Hold left click / touch HIT: attack with the equipped weapon.
1: stones. 2: switch. 3: heavy club. 4: stolen SWAT rifle.
F: punch. G: kick. Shift: sprint and body slam. Space: jump.
Q: smoke to break line of sight.
E: steal a dropped rifle, take ammo, pick up or drop a body, collect items,
   call/select the elevator, or use the service lift, depending on context.
When carrying a body, E drops it and Attack throws it.
C: cycle third-person, first-person, board and side views.
P / Escape: pause. Enter / Escape: skip the current cinematic.
Mouse wheel: zoom in board view.
Touch buttons and the left joystick provide the corresponding actions.
Gamepad: left stick movement, right stick look, A jump, X punch, B kick,
button 3 interact, button 10 sprint.

Interaction priority: drop a carried body, nearby rifle/ammo loot, nearby body,
elevator, service lift, normal item pickup. Loot requires an explicit E press.
The interaction prompt states what E will do.

3. WEAPONS, KILLS AND LOOT
--------------------------
A run starts with 100 HP, 16 stones, no rifle and no rifle ammunition.
All human characters have 100 HP. An attack with base damage of 100 or more
kills outright, ignoring temporary hit shields. Ordinary sub-100 damage can
be blocked by hit shields. Difficulty scales ordinary incoming player damage.
NPC damage is not reduced by the old 0.85 multiplier.
Police attacks can kill; lethal baton hits no longer force an arrest ending.

Stones: 52 damage, arcing projectile, 0.2-second base attack cooldown.
Switch: 15 character damage / 42 object damage, 0.15-second cooldown.
Heavy club: 46 character damage / 135 object damage, 0.6-second cooldown.
Punch: 24 damage. Kick: 42 damage. Sprint body slam: 18 damage.
Thrown bodies: 55 damage, credited to the player.

Rifle acquisition:
- Kill a SWAT officer with a credited player kill, then approach the drop.
- Press E to steal the rifle. Merely killing the officer does not equip it.
- Stealing supplies 30 rounds and equips the rifle; press 4 to re-equip it.
- Other deaths drop 12 rounds. Ammo may be collected before owning a rifle.
- A SWAT death not credited to the player supplies ammo, not a rifle unlock.
- Each drop can be collected once. Drops and weapon ownership reset per run.
- Loot falls to supported surfaces and can fall again after structural damage.

Rifle: 40 damage per hit, 180-metre range, 0.12-second base fire interval.
It consumes one round per shot and cannot fire with no ammunition.
Shots stop at the nearest solid target: cover, a character, an aircraft or a
vehicle. Aircraft and vehicle health is shown when aimed at with the rifle.
The rifle uses total carried ammunition, with no separate reload action.
SWAT officers visibly carry rifles and can also fire at range.

Credited kills increment the kill counter and fictional homicide ledger.
Officer kills award 1,600 base points; civilian/rival kills award 1,000.
Combo multipliers apply. Each victim can reward a kill only once.
Weapons, slams and thrown bodies award credit. A fatal environmental fall
within 10 seconds of player damage can also receive player credit.
Player-triggered structural collapse retains ownership for crush kills.

4. BUILDING SUPPORT, WOBBLE AND COLLAPSE
---------------------------------------
The tower is endless upward and streams a bounded set of nearby floors.
Floor height is 3.6 metres. Each floor has FOUR structural support columns.
"Structures left" in the collapse rule refers to those support columns,
not furniture, windows, wall panels or individual slab bays.
Each column has 2,400 HP regardless of floor height. CPU rivals do not target
columns. All non-player attacks (including police, vehicles, bombs and
gunship cannonballs) leave support columns undamaged. Player attacks and
player-credited destruction can still damage columns.

Four supports: normal frame.
Three or two supports: the upper tower remains anchored and gently tilts and
wobbles. Wobble is not a released collapse and does not cause crush damage.
One or zero supports: the upper section can be released into physical collapse.
Slab damage alone never releases the tower while two or more supports remain.
Destroyed slab bays still remove local floor support and can cause falls.

Wobble rotates the upper section around its supported base with bounded,
small oscillations. Collision/aiming proxies move with the tower. Wobble is
restored across floor-streaming updates. Catastrophic release removes the
anchor, disables the elevator and moves the loaded upper tower as a compound
rigid body. Slabs, walls and remaining columns participate in crush detection.
Swept bounds detect people in the path of moving structural pieces.
A moving collapse deals lethal 100-damage crush hits, including to the player.
A stationary settled section is not an unlimited damage zone.
Reset restores the frame, slab support, elevator and structural transforms.

5. AIRCRAFT, VEHICLES AND MILITARY RESPONSE
------------------------------------------
Gunships and troop transports each have 400 HP. Player stones and rifle fire
can damage them. At zero HP, the aircraft stops firing or deploying troops,
its warning marker is removed, and the wreck falls and spins toward support.
Shooting an aircraft down awards 5,000 base points once and increments the
aircraft-down counter. Crashing aircraft can kill people and damage objects.
Wrecks are cleaned up when they impact. Reset clears all aircraft state.

Gunships orbit the player and use fixed target warnings before firing.
Cannonballs are about 1.8 metres wide, travel at 110 m/s and last 4 seconds.
They sweep their full path to avoid tunnelling, deal instant-kill character
damage and 9,000 object damage (except protected support columns), and do
not stop at objects or people.
Ordinary modes allow two gunships. Insane allows sixteen. Siege raises the
ordinary cap with score, up to sixteen.

Transports fly in from outside the play area, hover at the player's selected
floor, deploy alternating SWAT/military units onto the exterior landing, then
leave. A normal deployment has four drops before mode pressure multipliers.
They do not deploy while crashing. Normal transport cap is two; Insane and
siege permit four.

Cars have 280 HP; SWAT trucks have 2,500 HP. Rifle fire can disable them.
A player-shot vehicle awards 2,500 base points on disable, once.
Vehicles charge, damage props, carry incoming squads and can bounce off pads.
Tanks have 1,200 HP and can also be disabled by rifle fire. Tanks and timed
bombing runs provide marked area attacks. Siege patterns
alternate carpet bombing, an artillery corridor and encirclement strikes.

6. THREAT HUD AND ENEMY TIERS
-----------------------------
DANGER Lx/5 is the response tier or the highest active enemy tier, whichever
is greater. Five illuminated bars correspond to that displayed level.
The live roster lists the tier, name and count of every active enemy type,
plus cars, trucks, gunships, airlifts and tanks. Downed aircraft do not count
as active threats. Floor height is a separate FLOOR display.

L1: POLICE (patrol).
L2: SCOUT, patrol cars.
L3: SWAT, RIOT, tactical trucks.
L4: SNIPER, MEDIC, gunships, troop airlifts.
L5: ARMY, GRENADIER, JUGGERNAUT, tanks.

Scouts move faster; riot units resist frontal forced melee hits; snipers use
telegraphed long-range shots; grenadiers use explosive projectiles; medics
heal nearby living allies; juggernauts have slower movement and heavy strikes.
All retain 100 HP. SWAT also has a visible shield and rifle.
Smoke blocks enemy tracking. Officers use the external switchback stairs.

7. SCORE, SPEED AND MODES
-------------------------
Combo multiplier: min(5, 1 + floor(combo / 3) * 0.5).
The multiplier reaches x5 at combo 24, not combo 12.
Destruction extends the chain window; kills also add combat points.
Score and kill rewards freeze after the run ends.

Every 500,000 points adds 15 percentage points of gameplay speed:
500K = 1.15x; 1M = 1.30x; 1.5M = 1.45x; 2M = 1.60x, continuing upward.
Movement, weapons, aircraft, physics and reinforcement pressure accelerate.
The match clock remains real-time and freezes when paused or in cinematics.

At 1,000,000, recurring combined siege waves begin: gunships, airlifts,
specialists, armor, vehicles and alternating bombardment patterns.
The base siege interval is 14 seconds divided by the current speed multiplier.
Spawn caps remain in place rather than allowing unlimited active entities.
Single Craze still applies its one-encounter-per-special-type rule.

Modes: Single Craze, Regular, Extreme and Insane.
Extreme starts at maximum response with an 8-second opening hit shield.
Insane starts with 12 gunships, up to 16, six tanks, mixed specialists and a
3-second opening hit shield. Insane has no passive healing.
Instant-kill cannonballs, fatal falls and lethal crush damage bypass shields.
The default match lasts five minutes; match length has a minimum of one
minute and no configured maximum. Ratings: D 0, C 10K, B 75K, A 300K,
S 1M, SS 3M, SSS 10M.

8. FALLS, BODIES, BLOOD AND ELEVATORS
------------------------------------
Falls of four storeys (14.4 m) or more cause a fatal splat on ground impact.
Trampolines can catch a genuine descending top contact before impact.
A splat removes the visible character and ragdoll bodies/joints completely.
Layered blood pools and fine droplets scale with fall height. Ground stains
persist for the entire round, including the ending, and reset on a new run.
Stains use four instanced texture batches; airborne spray is capped at 256.

Ordinary deaths leave ragdolls that can be picked up with E, physically held,
dropped with E or thrown with Attack. Thrown bodies can damage and kill.
At most eight ragdolls remain physical; held bodies are protected from eviction.
Unheld death ragdolls have a 60-second physical lifetime.

The floor-select elevator is at x=9, z=6 and moves at 1.25 m/s.
E opens its destination panel or calls the car from another floor.
It has 900 HP; weapons/explosions can destroy it and drop the wreck down the
shaft. It is also disabled by full tower collapse. Stairs remain the fallback.
The green service lift at x=21, z=8 launches the player about two floors up.

9. SETTINGS, ENDINGS AND STORAGE
--------------------------------
Graphics presets: Low, Medium, High, Ultra and Triple-A, with individual
controls for shadows, shadow resolution, effects, debris, distance, texture
detail, anisotropy, pixel ratio, saturation, exposure and post-processing.
Performance stats show FPS, frame time, draw calls, triangles and bodies.
One of five uploaded CE2 tracks is chosen per run and loops until ending.
Music and effects have independent volume and mute controls.

Intro plays each normal run. Timeout or banking leads to the surviving-player
ending; death uses a dedicated death cinematic and never revives the player.
Existing fictional arrest/Thursday story branches remain available to the
ending system, but lethal combat is not automatically converted to arrest.
Results include score, grade, destruction, kills, officers down, highest floor,
time, debt and a fictional crime ledger. Sentencing text is arcade fiction.

Settings and profile use localStorage key halfday-mayhem-v2. Records,
achievements and cosmetic unlocks persist; weapons, ammo and loot do not.

10. FILE MAP AND QA
-------------------
index.html: actual entry point; HUD, inventory, settings and ending markup.
style.css: responsive UI and graphics overlays.
game.js: loop, damage, rifle tracing, aircraft crashes, scoring, threat roster,
         streaming integration, input, settings, endings and acceptance tests.
loot.js: rifle model, dropped rifle/ammo collection and falling loot.
world.js: procedural tower/props, materials, streaming and entity models.
physics.js: Cannon world, anchored sway, collapse, crush detection, ragdolls,
            physical body carrying, particles, vehicle and trampoline bodies.
navigation.js: support lookup for intact/moving slabs and stair routing.
enemies.js: role stats, threat tiers and specialist equipment.
airlifts.js: transport approach, deployment, departure and crash handling.
bodies.js: body selection, physical carrying and thrown-body damage.
reinforcements.js: tanks, marked shells, bombs and siege patterns.
effects.js: instanced blood, bounded spray/debris, trails and ragdoll wrappers.
elevator.js: slow floor-select elevator, damage and reset.
config.js: modes, difficulty, ratings, milestones and graphics defaults.
crimes.js: fictional charge ledger, including credited homicide counts.
characters.js: procedural skinned meshes, animation and pose recovery.
cinematics.js: intro, death and surviving-player story sequences.
progression.js: saves, profile, achievements and cosmetics.
audio.js: soundtrack and procedural sound cues.
contacts.js: top-contact trampoline checks.
qa.html / qa.js: main-page-compatible automated acceptance harness.
assets/: generated world textures and four blood decal textures.
uploads/: soundtrack MP3 files.

Acceptance tests cover acquisition/loot/reset, rifle damage and cover,
shootable aircraft/vehicles, crash cleanup, every enemy's threat label,
anchored wobble and support threshold, lethal structural impacts, kills,
body carrying/throwing, falls/blood persistence, 500K speed steps and siege,
plus navigation, streaming, elevators, modes, physics, scoring and endings.
